How every metric is calculated — definitions & data notes
  • Net R — sum of per-trade R multiples exactly as logged. 1R = the trade's planned risk; EU$ and AU$ programmes risked 1% per trade.
  • Win rate — trades with R > 0 ÷ trades with R logged. Average R — net R ÷ trade count. Average win / loss — means of the positive and negative subsets.
  • Profit factor — gross winning R ÷ |gross losing R|. Max drawdown — largest peak-to-trough fall of the cumulative R curve, in R.
  • Coverage gaps, stated honestly: only 1 EU$ log row still has no R (4 Nov 2025 T2, an empty row) and is excluded everywhere. DAX direction is logged on 59 of 69 trades. The AU$ M1 EMs programme never logged direction at all. EU$ stop size exists for 70 of 76 trades; neither AU$ log recorded stop size, so no stop-size statistics are shown for AU$.
  • Backtest ≠ live. These are historical backtest results from research logs — not live or funded-account performance, and not a promise of future results.

EUR/USD — M5 London programme

Aug 2025 – May 2026 · London session · M5 entries · multi-timeframe order flow · backtest

+239.89RNet R
76Trades

+3.16RAvg R

Hypothesis

A liquidity sweep into a session point of interest, followed by a confirmed change of character (CHoCH), offers a repeatable London-session entry with asymmetric payoff — small, capped losses against runners.

Entry conditions

Sweep into session POI → confirmed CHoCH → entry on the programme's models: CC5 0.25 (38 trades, +107.18R), LC-1 (M5) (21, +80.16R), CC5- F (7, +16.20R), LQ-EM4 (4, +15.80R), M15 S-C (2, +6.20R). Strategy is logged on 72 of 76 trades — the 6 May 2026 trade's model was never named on its charts and is left blank rather than guessed.

Risk management

1% risk per trade. Average stop 6.8 pips (70 of 76 trades logged). Worst trade −1.00R; average loss −0.75R against a +3.96R average win.

What the data says

  • Probability grading works: my own pre-trade HP grade went 30 trades, 29 wins, +119.04R (avg +3.97R). LP averaged +1.85R and held 7 of the 13 losses — the grades order the outcomes exactly.
  • Phase A is premium: 9 trades, 88.9% win rate, avg +5.29R. Phase C is the leak: 36% of volume, 66.7% win rate, 9 of the 13 losses. Phase B lost only once in 24 trades (+83.74R).
  • Midday prints: the 12:00–12:30 window alone made +56.02R — 23% of the programme total — at 85.7%.
  • LC-1 quietly beat the flagship: 90.5% win rate vs CC5 0.25's 76.3%; CC5's full losses were unconfirmed entries.

Weaknesses & limitations

One trade (+15.66R, 25 Aug 2025) is 6.5% of the total; the top three are 14.6%. Wide stops (≥10 pips) underperformed (+2.31R avg vs +3.45R). Ten months and 76 trades is a promising sample, not proof.

RESEARCH / 002

DAX — “DAX 25” programme

Jan – Nov 2025 · London session · M5/M15 entries · every trade graded for decision quality · backtest

+202.17RNet R
69Trades
84.1%Win rate
+2.93RAvg R

Hypothesis

The same sweep → confirmation framework transfers to the DAX — but execution discipline, not the models, is the variable that decides the result.

Entry conditions

A completely different playbook from EU$ — no CC5 anywhere: OFS (31 trades, +76.44R), LC-1 (M5) (18, +52.06R), S-C (13, +42.75R, incl. the log's best trade +12.50R), LC-2A (M5) and LQ-EM4 (7 trades combined, 7 wins).

Risk management

Average stop 42.6 points (64 of 69 trades logged). Average win +3.66R vs average loss −0.93R; worst trade −1.00R. Every trade is colour-graded after review: gray = valid, red = invalid, green = over-aggressive, blue = no clear idea.

What the data says

  • Decision quality is the story: the 60 gray trades alone made +202.22R at 91.7% win rate, with a max loss streak of 1. The 9 non-gray trades net −0.05R combined — removing 9 trades changes the whole log.
  • March 2025 was the drawdown (44.4% win rate, the log's only 4-loss streak) — and 3 of those 4 streak losses were non-gray decisions, not model failures.
  • Phase A again: 24 trades, +99.30R — 49% of all DAX profit. Trading against order flow failed: Counter MTF OF averaged +0.84R vs Pro's +3.17R.
  • The open is dead time: 11:30–12:00 went 5W/5L (5 of the 11 losses); 12:00–13:00 made +121.46R.

Weaknesses & limitations

OFS is the workhorse but carries 7 of the 11 losses at 77.4%. Direction is logged on only 59 of 69 trades; news-day data is almost entirely absent (5 trades) and deliberately unused. Backtest only.

Hypothesis

The sweep → confirmation framework on AUD/USD, run as its own book — an LC-1-dominated programme rather than a copy of the EU$ playbook.

Entry conditions

LC-1 (M5) carries the book (18 of 23 trades, +45.85R), with LC-2A (M5) (3 trades, +9.30R) and M5 CE (1 trade, +5.10R). Best trade +7.76R (4 Jun 2025, LC-1 long).

Risk management

1% risk per trade. Every one of the 4 losses was exactly −1.00R — never cut early, never beyond plan. This log's template recorded no stop sizes, so none are claimed.

Weaknesses & limitations

April–May was the soft patch (May: 66.7% win rate). 23 trades across six months is a modest sample, and with no stop-size data, risk geometry can't be analysed for this programme.

Hypothesis

A short M1 experiment: can expansion-model entries on the one-minute timeframe produce outsized winners within the same session framework?

Entry conditions

Models found in no other log: LQ-EM2 (6 trades, +17.90R), LQ-EM4 (2 trades, +19.10R, incl. the +13.00R best trade on 8 Jan 2025) and CE .618 (1 trade, +4.00R). Average win +6.14R.

1% risk per trade; both losses were exactly −1.00R. Longest win streak 4, longest loss streak 1.

Weaknesses & limitations

Nine trades over five weeks: every statistic from this programme is small-sample and is labelled that way wherever it appears. Direction was never logged in this programme, so no direction split exists. It was an experiment, not a validated system.

Cross-programme findings
WHAT SURVIVES ACROSS ALL FOUR LOGS
  • Losses are structurally capped; winners run. Worst trade across all 177: −1R. Payoff asymmetry (avg win ÷ |avg loss|) is ≈5.3× on EU$ and ≈3.9× on DAX.
  • Pre-trade grading predicts outcomes on EU$ (HP +3.97R avg → MP +3.43R → LP +1.85R) and DAX (HP +3.42R → LP +1.47R) — the journal's own labels carry signal.
  • Time of day is an edge variable, not a detail: midday London windows dominate every log, and the weak windows differ per instrument.
  • Context does not transfer blindly. Phase A is premium on EU$/DAX and the weakest context on AU$ M5/M15 — each book has to be read on its own data.

04 / Research methodology

Process before performance.

A disciplined research process separates a repeatable trading hypothesis from an attractive result that may simply be noise. A promising backtest is a hypothesis that survived one dataset; a validated system also needs out-of-sample behaviour and live execution evidence. By that standard, everything here is honestly presented as research-stage.

Risk is managed structurally: fixed fractional risk (1% on the EU$ and AU$ programmes), stops placed at invalidation, and decision-quality grading that measures trades taken outside a stable process separately instead of quietly averaging them in.

01

Observe

Watch how price behaves around session liquidity and points of interest; identify recurring behaviour and structural patterns.

02

Form a hypothesis

Translate observations into explicit, falsifiable trading rules — including what result would kill the idea.

03

Collect and validate data

Log every candidate trade with model, grade, phase, window, stop size and R. Check time alignment, missing values and biases.

04

Backtest

Apply the rules consistently over history — London session only, fixed risk — and document every assumption.

05

Evaluate statistically

Split results by model, phase, grade, weekday and window. Measure expectancy, drawdown, streaks and robustness — and hunt the leaks.

06

Refine

Cut what the data condemns: unconfirmed entries, weak phases, dead time windows. Keep what survives contact with the splits.

07

Document

Record limitations, improvements and what the evidence does not establish — so the next test starts from evidence, not memory.

06 / About

Research first. Opinions last.

I'm Rajwardhan Kumar, an independent trader based in Barh, Bihar, India, trading since August 2024. I work one session — London — across EUR/USD, AUD/USD and the DAX, because depth on a narrow problem beats breadth I can't verify.

My work is a loop: observe market behaviour, write the hypothesis down, test it against logged data, and let the splits — by model, phase, grade and time window — decide what survives. The results above include the uncomfortable findings (a flagship model beaten by a quieter one, a drawdown caused by decision quality rather than the system), because those are the findings that improve the next test.

Alongside trading, I support other traders as a community moderator, and I'm building toward quantitative development — learning Python properly, with the aim of writing my own backtesting and analysis tooling.
